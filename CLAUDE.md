# ONTOK

ONTOK exists so that meaning is commensurable without a mind to reconcile it.

Two systems modeling the same world will never agree about what exists. Every universal kernel that
tried to reach commensurability through agreement about the world failed there, because that is the
one thing two domains cannot concede to each other. What they can agree on is what a statement is
doing, which costs neither of them anything and is already true of both. So ONTOK classifies
statements, not things, and it is a notation applied to ontologies other people already built rather
than an ontology anyone adopts.

A statement has three positions: subject, predicate, value. There is no fourth. When a statement
holds, what scopes it, and what it rests on are not positions, because a fact carries several
sources and holds in several scopes at once, and a position holds one. Each of those is a statement
about the statement.

A position holds a referent, a literal, a statement, a kind, or nothing. The line between the first
two is the whole of it: two ontologies can disagree whether their Acme is one Acme, and they cannot
disagree whether their 34 is one 34. A referent needs a correspondence asserted. A literal does not.

There are twelve kinds, and which one a statement is follows from what its positions hold. Entity,
event, state, role and relation hold referents and literals, and stand to the world. Claim,
evidence, derivation, invalidation, rule and context hold a statement, and stand to what is said.
Concept holds a kind, and is where a domain meets this model.

Of those, claim, evidence and derivation ground a statement: assertion, artifact, inference. There
is no fourth ground. Invalidation releases a ground rather than supplying one, which is why a
withdrawn statement is still there and still answers, and why what currently holds is computed and
never stored.

The kind of a statement is the kind its predicate's concept reaches. Classify a predicate once and
every statement using it takes its kind. That is what makes transcription mechanical instead of
interpretive, and it is the reason ONTOK can be applied by someone who is not in the room.

ONTOK states nothing about what exists, names no predicate, and assumes no substrate. Any of the
three would make it something to be adopted, and an adopted thing is not commensurable with the
things that did not adopt it.
